    Constraints¶

    The rules that shape what we can build (see rule-analysis.md):

    • Start size: 18" x 18" x 18" (SG1)
    • Horizontal expansion: never bigger than 24" x 24" footprint at any point (SG2)
    • Vertical expansion: never taller than 50" (SG3); this applies the whole match, so during the 1:45 we can go tall freely up to 50"
    • SG12 (soft vertical cap) only applies in the ENDGAME midfield: the last 0:10, and only for robots in the midfield. It does not restrict tall stacking during normal play.
    • Possession: max 1 pin + 1 cup at once (SG6), the big constraint on hoarding
    • Preload: 1 pin (SG5)
    • Motors: 55W combined on subsystem 2; climbing mechanisms are outside that limit but can't share motors with the drive (R11b); see rule-analysis
    • Don't touch opposing alliance goals (SG9) or opposing load zones in driver control (SG13)
    • Match loads only enter through your own loaders, driver control only (SG11)

    Idea expansion¶

    Climbing bot¶

    Climb the stack to place from the top. Climbing isnt not allowed During normal play we can be tall (up to 50", SG3); But climbing lets us make really tall towers makeing the risk of loseing toggles lower (dont need to keep as many toggles) Main cost is placement speed from height vs. cycle time.

    Lifted by sister-team bot¶

    Partner lifts us to reach height or reposition without carrying our own lift mass. No rule against it. Cost is coordination (both robots committed), but the "free height / free reposition" same advantages as climbing

    Shooting / tossing bot¶

    = throwing one pin or cup on top of a stack. Under SG6 we can possess 1 at a time, and there's no rule against releasing/tossing a scoring object as long as it ends up placed (nested). So this is legal; the hard part is precision (landing it nested + keeping the scoring half visible), not very feasable, but also not impossable.

    Flying bot¶

    Not technically illegal: no rule says the robot must stay on the ground (S4 only penalizes being fully outside the field). The fun is the math: can a V5-legal robot generate enough lift/thrust within the 24"x24"x50" envelope? TODO: do the physics, thrust-to-weight with V5 motors + props made from legal vex parts, is it even close? Probably not, but it would be fun to do the math :3

    Jump bot¶

    Technically possible if you "get gud": a spring / stored-energy / pneumatic hop to clear traffic or reach the midfield. Pneumatics are limited (R25/R26) and it's risky, but not banned. Feasibility question: how much height can we get from legal stored energy?

    Scissor lift¶

    Legal, reaches stack height. Slow, and watch the 24" footprint as it extends (SG2). Classic but cycle-time-heavy.

    Fast bot¶

    Cycle time is the binding constraint (see the max score analysis); raw speed = points. Strong baseline that any of the above can be layered onto.

    Where the points actually are (from the max score analysis)¶

    1. Yellow pins are 2x alliance-color pins, but only when owned; ownership comes from toggles (per quadrant) and midfield robot majority (endgame). Control ownership → the yellow value is yours instead of theirs. Massive swings.
    2. Cycle time beats stack height. SG6's 1-pin-1-cup cap means every score is a round trip. A fast, reliable placer that keeps the scoring half visible outscores a tall stacker.
    3. Endgame midfield = 8 pts/robot + the yellow-midfield ownership tiebreaker.
