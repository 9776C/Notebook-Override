    Game Analysis¶

    Identify¶

    We need to understand Override well enough to decide the most effective way to score. The plan is to work out the absolute maximum score the game allows, then look at what that maximum is made of. Whatever makes up most of the ceiling is the most point-dense method available, so that's what we design around.

    The game. Override (V5RC 2026-2027) is played on a 12' x 12' square field. Head-to-head matches are 2v2: a red alliance and a blue alliance, two teams each.

    Match structure:

    • Autonomous Period: 0:15 (no drivers)
    • Driver Controlled Period: 1:45
    • Endgame: the last 0:10 of driver control, "king of the hill" in the midfield

    The object: score more than the other alliance by (1) stacking Pins and Cups onto Goals, (2) setting Toggles to your color to own yellow pins in that quadrant, and (3) ending the match with your Robot in the contested Midfield.

    Scoring:

    Action Points
    Autonomous Bonus (1 alliance) 12
    Each scored alliance-color pin half 5
    Each scored yellow pin half (owned) 10
    Each robot in the Midfield (end) 8

    Plus the Autonomous Win Point (ranking WP, not match points) for completing the auton task set with no violations.

    Appendix - Field Overview

    Field Overview

    The V5RC Override Field consists of the following:

    • 56 Cups
      • 20 Match Loads
        • 10 for red Alliance
        • 10 for blue Alliance
      • 24 that start the Match in predetermined locations on the Field (gray side up)
      • 12 that start the Match in predetermined locations on the Field (clear side up)
    • 63 Pins
      • 4 red/blue that start the Match in predetermined locations
      • 20 red/yellow
        • 2 Preloads
        • 10 Match Loads
        • 8 that start the Match in predetermined locations
      • 20 blue/yellow
        • 2 Preloads
        • 10 Match Loads
        • 8 that start the Match in predetermined locations
      • 19 yellow/yellow
        • 2 Match Loads
        • 17 that start the Match in predetermined locations
    • 9 Goals
      • 4 Alliance colored
        • 2 red
        • 2 blue
      • 5 neutral colored
        • 4 short
        • 1 tall
    • 4 Toggles
    • 4 Loaders, two adjacent to each Alliance Station

    A few things stand out about how this game works:

    • Toggles are a fast, cheap way to swing a lot of points, since flipping one lets you own all the yellow in a quadrant
    • Possession is capped at 1 pin + 1 cup, which caps how fast you can score more than it caps how much.
    • The last 10 seconds in the midfield decide both the 8pt/robot swing and who owns the midfield yellow.

    Brainstorm¶

    Max Score¶

    What is the absolute ceiling?

    Counting the pin halves. Each pin has 2 halves. 63 pins → 126 halves. The pin/cup/goal counts come from the Field Overview embedded above:

    Pin type Count Red halves Blue halves Yellow halves
    red/blue 4 4 4 0
    red/yellow 20 20 0 20
    blue/yellow 20 0 20 20
    yellow/yellow 19 0 0 38
    total 63 24 24 78

    Cups do the covering. A half only scores while it stays visible, and the thing that hides a half is a cup: each cup joins two stacked pins and covers exactly one of the two halves meeting it, whichever one sits in its opaque side. Rotate the cup and you pick which. That's what saves the red/blue pins: they have no yellow half, but you can still hide them completely by seating each between two cups with the opaque sides facing in, so both halves vanish and the neighbors show yellow instead. We have 56 cups against just 48 non-yellow halves (24 + 24), so nothing but yellow ever has to show.

    So how many halves stay visible? Covering costs cups, and cups are also what let you stack. To place all 63 pins you build them onto the 7 goals you can reach (your 2 + 5 neutral; the opponent's 2 are off-limits). 63 pins in 7 stacks takes 63 − 7 = 56 cup joints, and each joint hides one half. So 56 halves get buried and 126 − 56 = 70 stay visible — all of them yellow (owning them needs all 4 toggle quadrants + the midfield):

    Source Points
    70 shown yellow halves (10 each) 700
    2 robots in the midfield (8 each) 16
    Autonomous bonus 12
    Absolute max (1 alliance) 728

    Select¶

    The number itself matters less than what it's made of:

    • 700 of the 728 points (96%) are yellow. Every non-yellow half gets buried under a cup, even both halves of the red/blue pins, so nothing but yellow shows. Yellow is really the whole scoring plan, and alliance-color halves are just the fallback when you can't get yellow up.
    • Yellow only counts when you own it, and ownership comes from toggles (per quadrant) and midfield majority (endgame). That makes a toggle flip the most point-dense action in the game: one flip turns a whole quadrant of yellow from 0 to 10 each, and zeroes out the opponent's yellow there at the same time. It swings both ways at once.
    • The real limit is cycle time, not stack height. SG6 caps possession at 1 pin + 1 cup, so every score is a round trip. What we're actually short on is owned-yellow placements per second.

    Chosen scoring method (ranked by points-per-action):

    1. Flip toggles → own a quadrant of yellow (huge swing, one cheap action)
    2. Place yellow pins in quadrants/midfield you own (10/half)
    3. Hold the midfield at endgame (8/robot + owns midfield yellow + tiebreaker)
    4. Alliance-color pins (5/half) only when yellow/toggles aren't available
